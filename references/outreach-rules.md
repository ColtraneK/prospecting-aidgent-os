# Outreach rules — value first, and the code checks the quotes

You draft `suggested_comment` and `suggested_intro`; the grounding validator
checks them before they become cells. These are the rules that make a draft
worth sending, and the checks it must survive.

## The first touch gives, it never asks

- **Give something tied to their signal.** React to the specific thing they
  said: extend it, answer the question they asked, add one concrete
  experience of your own. The value must be legible in the message itself.
- **Never ask for a call in the first message.** No "quick chat", no
  "15 minutes", no calendar link. The first touch earns the second.
- **Never "came across your profile"** — or any cousin of it ("stumbled
  upon", "found you while browsing"). It announces automation.
- **A question only invites reaction to the value you gave.** "Curious how
  you handle the handoff after that" works; "want to see how we do it?" is a
  pitch with a question mark.
- Never mention AI, automation, or this tool. Never imply you know them
  beyond the post you just read — you have not met them, you do not follow
  them, you did not read their newsletter.

## The specificity bar

**If a draft could fit 100 people, redraft it.** "Loved your post on
operations" fits everyone; "your line that capacity is the constraint nobody
budgets for is the bit I keep coming back to" fits one person. The test:
could this message be sent, unchanged, under a different post? Then it says
nothing.

## What the code enforces (and will not negotiate)

- **Grounding = quote 4+ consecutive words of the captured post** (or the
  whole post when it is shorter than four words). This is the
  anti-fabrication check: fluency cannot pass it, only having read the post.
- Length: DM ≤ 280 characters, comment ≤ 250.
- No URLs, no `|` (headline paste), no mid-word ellipsis.
- The greeting must match the first name in column A.
- **With no captured post: claim nothing about them.** You may say who you
  work with and ask for their view — their take, perspective, read — and
  that is all. No "your recent post", no "saw you have been…". A comment
  cannot exist at all in that state; there is nothing to comment on.
- A failing draft is blanked and reported for redraft. It is never repaired,
  never "close enough", and the reason never goes into their Notes column.

## Voice

Use the persona's `voice`. Warm, concise, curious, no pitch. One idea per
message. Write like a peer who read the post, because that is the only thing
that is true.
